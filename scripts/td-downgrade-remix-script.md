## SCENE 3 — The part nobody explains: what moves YOUR rate
[WORD-FOR-WORD]

This is where people get confused, so let me make it simple.

There are two different things that move your mortgage rate. And they don't move together.

If you have a variable rate, it follows the Bank of Canada. When they cut or hold, your rate follows.

If you have a fixed rate, it follows the bond market. Specifically, the five-year Government of Canada bond yield. Bond yields can rise even when the Bank of Canada does nothing. That's why you'll sometimes see banks raise fixed rates and everybody asks, what happened? Nobody touched the rate. The bond market moved.

So when someone says the housing market is weakening, that does not automatically mean your fixed rate drops. It might. It might not. Housing forecasts and your rate are two different dials.

## SCENE 7 — The five-minute renewal plan
[WORD-FOR-WORD]

So here's what I'd actually do. Five steps.

One. Find your renewal date. Most lenders will send an offer about four months out, but you can start shopping as early as you like.

Two. Pull your balance and your current rate. You need your real numbers.

Three. Ask for quotes from at least two other lenders, plus a broker who can see many at once.

Four. Take the best offer back to your current lender and ask them to match it.

Five. Decide on term and type based on how much payment risk you can handle, not on a headline. If a rate jump of a point or two would hurt, a longer fixed term might let you sleep. If you have a cushion and want flexibility, a shorter or variable term can work.

And the thing most people skip? Step four. Asking. It costs you nothing, and lenders will move more often than you'd think.
