Erindale sits on the east bank of the Credit River in south-central
Mississauga, built up around the old crossing of Dundas Street West and
the river itself. It's one of the oldest settled parts of the city, and
one that went through more name changes than almost any other local
village before settling on the one it has today.

## A short history

The land here opened for settlement in 1822, when speculator Thomas Racey
bought the tract along the Credit River following an 1820 land purchase
in which the Mississaugas ceded land on both sides of the river to the
Crown. Racey's plans for mills on the site ran into financial trouble,
and the land was broken up and auctioned off to settlers in 1825.

The resulting village went by several names before its current one
stuck. Early accounts call it Toronto, then Credit, then Springfield, and
eventually Springfield-on-the-Credit, a name tied to the water-powered
mills that the river's bend made possible. The Credit Valley Railway
reached the village in 1879, and around 1900 residents renamed it
Erindale, after the estate of Reverend James Magrath, who drew on "Erin"
as a nod to Ireland. A fire in 1919 destroyed much of the old village
core.

In the early 1900s, a hydroelectric company dammed the Credit River at
Erindale, creating a reservoir known as Lake Erindale to power a plant on
the site. The dam was later decommissioned, and its ruins are still
visible today along the trails of Erindale Park. Erindale joined the
other villages of Toronto Township in 1968 to form the Town of
Mississauga, which became a city in 1974 after absorbing Streetsville
and Port Credit.

## Getting there

**Erindale GO Station**, on the Milton line near Mississauga Road and
Rathburn Road, sits close to the University of Toronto Mississauga
campus on the river's west bank; see our
[Erin Mills guide](/guides/erin-mills-neighbourhood-guide/) for more on
that side of the Credit. Service on the Milton line runs mainly during
weekday rush hours rather than all day, so check GO's schedule before
planning a trip. MiWay buses connect the area to the rest of the city;
see our [transit guide](/guides/getting-around-mississauga-transit/) for
the wider picture.

## What's there today

- **Erindale Park.** A large wooded park that follows the Credit River
  through the neighbourhood, with trails, picnic areas, and the ruins of
  the old hydro dam along the water. It's one of the bigger green spaces
  in this part of the city.
- **University of Toronto Mississauga.** Opened as Erindale College in
  September 1967 on the river's west bank, the campus now serves
  thousands of students and sits close enough to the old village that
  the two names, Erindale and the college, are still used almost
  interchangeably by longtime residents.
- **Local shops and services along Dundas Street West.** Browse our
  [restaurant directory](/directory/restaurants/) for what's currently
  open and highly rated nearby.

## Good to know

- Sources disagree on the exact year the Erindale name took hold, with
  dates ranging from the 1890s to 1900; treat any single year you see
  elsewhere as approximate.
- Erindale Park's old dam ruins are a notable feature but aren't
  maintained as a formal historic site, so visit on foot via the park's
  trails rather than expecting signage or guided access.
