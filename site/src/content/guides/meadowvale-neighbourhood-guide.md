Meadowvale sits in northwest Mississauga, split between two places that
share a name but tell very different stories. Meadowvale Village, a small
cluster of streets along the Credit River, is one of the oldest surviving
settlements in the city. Modern Meadowvale, the much larger residential
community built up around it, dates back only to the 1970s.

## A short history

The story starts in 1819, when a caravan of Irish immigrant families set
out from New York State under John Beatty, a total of 54 adults and 33
children travelling in roughly 26 wagons. Most of the group, led by John
and his brother David Beatty, settled along the Credit River in what
became Meadowvale, while a smaller party continued on to found what is
now Derry West. Land grants of 200 acres each went to the Beatty
brothers, William Wheeler, and Joseph Carter.

The settlement grew into a formally recognized village by 1836, with
James Crawford, John Simpson, and Francis Silverthorn establishing the
milling tradition that came to define the community. By the 1860s,
Meadowvale Village had a general store, post office, blacksmiths, a
cheese factory, two sawmills, and a large grist mill. The Toronto
distilling family behind Gooderham and Worts moved in starting in 1860,
and their grist mill was turning out as much as 300 barrels of flour a
day at its peak.

Meadowvale's fortunes as a milling centre faded after 1879, when the
Credit Valley Railway was built through the region and bypassed the
village entirely. Several buildings from that earlier era still stand
today, including the 1840 Hill House, one of the oldest homes in the
village, and the 1863 church at Derry Road and Second Line, now a United
Church. In 1980, the City of Mississauga designated Meadowvale Village as
Ontario's first Heritage Conservation District, a status that today
protects 63 structures along Second Line West and Old Derry Road, 23 of
them recognized as individually significant.

The much larger neighbourhood that carries the Meadowvale name today grew
up separately, starting in 1970, when developer Markborough Properties
began building a planned community on former farmland west of the
historic village. Meadowvale Town Centre, the development's shopping hub,
opened on January 25, 1978, with 21 stores including what was then the
largest Dominion supermarket in Canada, and grew to 51 stores within two
years. A major redevelopment in 2002 tore down most of the enclosed mall
and rebuilt it as the open-air shopping centre that stands there now.

## Getting there

**Meadowvale GO Station**, on GO Transit's Milton line near Derry Road
West and Winston Churchill Boulevard, mainly runs weekday, rush-hour
focused train service rather than all-day or weekend rail, the same
pattern as the rest of the Milton line through Streetsville and Erin
Mills. Off-peak and weekend travellers typically rely on GO buses or
MiWay buses instead. See our
[transit guide](/guides/getting-around-mississauga-transit/) for the
wider picture.

## What's there

- **Meadowvale Town Centre.** The neighbourhood's commercial hub, with
  several restaurants rated highly in our own data: Swiss Chalet at 4.9
  from over 4,600 reviews and Taps Public House at 4.8 from over 5,300
  reviews. Browse our [restaurant directory](/directory/restaurants/) for
  the fuller, current picture.
- **Meadowvale Community Centre.** A city recreation facility on Glen
  Erin Drive, rated 4.5 from over 2,400 reviews in our
  [fitness & sports directory](/directory/fitness/).
- **Meadowvale Village Heritage Conservation District.** The historic
  core along Second Line West and Old Derry Road, home to the 1840 Hill
  House, the 1863 church, and other 19th-century buildings protected as
  Ontario's first heritage district.
- **Meadowvale Conservation Area.** A natural area along the Credit River
  at the end of Second Line West, with the Glassford Trail running
  through it; see our [Things to Do](/events/) page for current
  programming nearby.

## Good to know

- Meadowvale Village and modern Meadowvale are two distinct places
  despite sharing a name, so don't expect the heritage district's
  19th-century streetscape when visiting the newer residential
  subdivisions further west, or vice versa.
- Meadowvale Town Centre's original enclosed mall was almost entirely
  demolished in a 2002 redevelopment, so little remains today of the
  shopping centre that opened in 1978.
- The heritage conservation district's boundaries are fairly small and
  residential, and people still live in the protected historic homes
  along Second Line West and Old Derry Road, worth keeping in mind if you
  visit.
