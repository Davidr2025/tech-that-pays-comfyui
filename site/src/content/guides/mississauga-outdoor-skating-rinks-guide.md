Once the weather turns, Mississauga's outdoor rinks become one of the
easiest free things to do in the city. There are two different kinds,
though, and knowing which is which saves a wasted trip.

## Two kinds of free rink

The city runs a small number of **artificial outdoor rinks**, including
the rink at **Celebration Square** in City Centre, which also doubles as
the site's main public plaza for concerts and festivals the rest of the
year. These are refrigerated, so they're less dependent on the weather
once the season is running.

Alongside those, the city supports a much larger network of **natural
rinks**, flooded and maintained by neighbourhood volunteers in local
parks rather than by city crews. The exact number varies from year to
year depending on how many volunteers sign up and how the weather
cooperates, typically somewhere in the two dozen to thirty range. The
city supplies some of the infrastructure, including rink boards and a
heated hut at each site, while volunteers handle the actual ice
maintenance through the season.

Both kinds are free to skate on. Bring your own skates either way; skate
rentals, where available at all, are limited to the artificial rinks
rather than the volunteer-run ones.

## When the season actually starts

Outdoor ice in Mississauga is weather-dependent, so there's no fixed
opening date. Natural rinks in particular typically don't open until
late December or into January, once temperatures have stayed cold enough
for long enough to build and hold ice, and a mild stretch can push that
back further. The artificial rinks are less exposed to this, but the
whole outdoor skating season still runs on a winter clock rather than a
calendar one.

Because of that, the most reliable way to know what's actually open on
any given day is to check the city's own outdoor rinks page rather than
go by a date you saw somewhere else, since conditions can change
week to week. The city's general information line, 311, can also confirm
current status if you're heading out and want to double check first.

Rather than list every park here, since the roster of natural rinks

## Good to know

- Natural rinks run on volunteer time. If a rink near you is listed but
  the ice looks rough or isn't flooded yet, that's usually a volunteer
  and weather question, not a city maintenance failure.
- The city periodically opens applications for new natural rink
  volunteers; if you're interested in helping maintain one in your own
  park next season, that's worth watching for on the city's site.
- None of this is attached to specific businesses, so if you're also
  looking for somewhere to warm up afterward, our
  [café & bakery directory](/directory/cafes/) is a good place to start.
