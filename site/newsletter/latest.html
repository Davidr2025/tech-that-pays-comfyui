Spending under review as Peel chair sets priorities for Mississauga, Brampton, Caledon
Affordability will be the top priority for Peel Region’s returning chair Nando Iannicca as he begins a new four-year term, with a review of regional services and spending expected to shape next year’s budget. Iannicca made the commitment Thursday (Nov. 19) during his inaugural address to the newly elected Peel Regional Council, saying residents and businesses have made it clear that the rising cost of living must be at the centre of the region’s decisions. The Ontario government reappointed Iannicca to the position for the 2026-2030 term, making him the only one of eight regional and upper-tier chairs across the province to be retained following the recent municipal election. But he faces pressure to make affordability more than just a talking point. Last week, the mayors of Mississauga, Brampton and Caledon issued a joint appeal urging Iannicca to make reducing the financial burden on residents a priority as he prepares his first budget of the new term. Iannicca said he has heard that message and is prepared to make some difficult decisions. “I’ve spoken to the mayors, and I thank you after our conversations to pen a letter to me in effect saying, ‘Chair, we know the responsibility. We are urging you to take it seriously and strongly,'” he told council.

Federal Housing Fund Backs $1 Million in Mississauga Rebates for Basement and Garage Conversions
According to the City of Mississauga, homeowners can tap more than $1 million in funding through the city's Gentle Density Incentive Program to help cover the cost of adding new licensed units to their properties. The city says the incentives apply to units such as basement apartments, garage conversions, garden suites, triplexes and fourplexes, lowering costs for homeowners creating new housing. Mississauga reports it has issued more than $839,000 in gentle density incentives so far, funded through the federal Housing Accelerator Fund. In most cases, the city says homeowners building one or two units automatically get a rebate covering permit fees once construction is complete. The city notes homeowners building a fourplex may qualify for extra incentives, and it is promoting the program ahead of National Housing Day. The funding is available for a limited time, according to the city.

Brampton Assault Call Leads Police to Fatal Standoff at UTM Student Residence
According to the SIU, Peel police received a 911 call Monday afternoon after a man allegedly assaulted a woman in Brampton, and officers located him later that evening at a student residence on the University of Toronto Mississauga campus near Dundas Street West and Mississauga Road. The watchdog says a gunshot was heard and the 19 year old man was found with an apparent self-inflicted gunshot wound, and he was pronounced dead early Tuesday morning. Before the shooting was confirmed, UTM issued an alert telling students to shelter in place because of police activity near the McLuhan Court residence, according to the university. Ontario's Special Investigations Unit has opened an investigation into the circumstances of the death, the SIU said. UTM said Tuesday it was saddened by the death and the investigation, and confirmed the campus would stay open while cancelling Tuesday classes.

Prologis and Montcrest Asset Management have filed separate appeals of Mississauga’s freeze on large data centres, putting a stalled 40-MW project in Meadowvale and the future use of a property near Pearson airport before the Ontario Land Tribunal. Council adopted the freeze it proposed in July on Sept. 16. The bylaw pauses new facilities, conversions and expansions with peak power demand of 10 MW or more for up to a year. Mississauga is among the largest Canadian municipalities to impose a pause as local councils grapple with how to regulate large data centres. Neighbouring Oakville adopted its own temporary restrictions in August, and Calgary is weighing a pause of its own. Mississauga to Host December Open House on $500M Plan for Third MiWay Bus Garage
According to insauga.com, Mississauga will host a public open house on Dec. 8 to present plans for a new transit facility near Mississauga Road and Meadowvale Boulevard that would become MiWay's third bus garage. The outlet reports MiWay keeps its roughly 500 buses at two facilities, one in the Erindale area and one in Malton, both of which are at full capacity and cannot accommodate additional buses. City officials said the new site would need to hold between 170 and 280 buses, with an estimated capital cost of more than $500 million, according to insauga.com. Former mayor Carolyn Parrish wrote to the federal government earlier this year seeking funding, warning that without a new facility the city's shift to zero emission buses would be constrained, the outlet reported. Insauga.com notes the facility is meant to support the city's Climate Change Action Plan.
