Only Top Four Mayoral Candidates Will Take Part in Oct. 1 Mississauga Debate
According to Mississauga.com, Food Banks Mississauga and the Metamorphosis Network will host a debate for the city's mayoral candidates on October 1 ahead of the October 26 municipal election. The outlet reports the event is organized to give candidates a chance to address income, housing security and food availability issues facing residents. According to a statement from the organizers, only the four candidates who are polling highest will be invited to take part, with the field confirmed by September 18. Mississauga.com reports the public can submit questions for the debate through an anonymous survey covering topics they want addressed, along with candidates' policy commitments and goals for their first year in office. The outlet reports that survey will also remain open until September 18. According to Mississauga.com, public registration for the debate itself opens in September, giving residents time to secure a spot before the event.

Cooksville Development Would Swap Two Houses for a Dozen Townhomes Near the QEW
According to INsauga, a new development application submitted to the City of Mississauga proposes replacing two houses in Cooksville, north of the QEW, with 12 townhomes. The outlet reports the existing homes, along with any sheds or other structures on the properties, would be demolished to make way for the project, though the developer hopes to preserve mature trees along the rear and side lot lines. INsauga reports the townhomes would be split between two blocks flanking a common element condominium roadway, with access from Harborn Road. According to the outlet, five units are planned for the block on the west side of the private road and seven units for the block on the east side, with each townhome getting its own driveway and single-car garage. INsauga reports the driveways would have enough room to park an additional vehicle, and four visitor parking spaces, including one accessible space, are planned at the south end of the roadway.

Thief Takes $1,000 Meant for Malton Food Bank Clients and Domestic Violence Survivors
According to Mississauga.com, someone broke into the Seva Food Bank's Malton location in the early hours of August 23 and stole about $1,000 in cash and gift cards. The outlet reports the break-in was discovered later that afternoon when volunteers dropping off donated items noticed an open window and alerted staff. Mississauga.com quotes a food bank representative identified as Saini, who said the volunteers already had access to the entryway, let themselves in, noticed the window open and immediately called staff. According to the outlet, the stolen cash had been set aside for purchases at the food bank, while the gift cards were meant to support survivors of domestic and intimate partner violence. Mississauga.com reports the incident has left staff concerned about what they describe as a violation of their safe space.

New Pedestrian Bridge in Caledon Marks Milestone for 100-Kilometre Credit Valley Trail
According to INsauga, a new 40-metre pedestrian bridge has opened along the developing Credit Valley Trail at the Upper Credit Conservation Area in Caledon, marking a milestone in the decades-long project to build a 100-kilometre trail linking Mississauga's Lake Ontario shoreline to communities north of the Greater Toronto Area. The outlet reports Credit Valley Conservation, which owns the property, called the bridge a major infrastructure project connecting the trail from the Credit River headwaters in Orangeville down to Port Credit in Mississauga. INsauga quotes Terri Hamilton, the conservation authority's chief administrative officer, saying the bridge marks an important step in bringing the vision of the Credit Valley Trail to life by connecting it across the Credit River. According to the outlet, the overall project began nearly a decade ago and is scheduled for completion in 2041. INsauga reports the trail currently spans 43 kilometres of its planned 100-kilometre length, with Credit Valley Conservation describing it as steadily taking shape.

Free Author Talks and Workshops Return for Mississauga's Third Annual Literary Festival
According to the City of Mississauga, the third annual Mississauga Literary Festival will take place September 12 at Hazel McCallion Central Library, offering free family activities, workshops and author talks celebrating books and writing. The city says the event, hosted by Mississauga Library, is designed to give guests of all ages engaging ways to connect with the community and build a lifelong love of reading. According to the city, the festival includes something for readers and writers of every age, with all events and activities free to attend on a first-come, first-served basis. The city notes space is limited, so attendees are encouraged to arrive early, and residents can register on Eventbrite to receive updates. According to the city, the library is close to several MiWay bus routes, and officials suggest using the MiWay Trip Planner as an alternative to driving and paying for gas.

33-Year-Old Recovering After Overnight Stabbing in Lakeview, Police Say No Ongoing Threat
According to INsauga, a 33-year-old man suffered a non-life-threatening stab wound early Thursday morning in the area of Cawthra Road and Lakeshore Road East in Lakeview. The outlet reports police believe the injury was sustained during an altercation that occurred sometime overnight. INsauga reports the man was transported to a local hospital by ambulance for treatment. According to the outlet, investigators are still working to determine the exact circumstances surrounding the incident and how the injury was sustained. Police told INsauga there is no information at this time to suggest an ongoing threat to public safety, and no suspect information has been released.
