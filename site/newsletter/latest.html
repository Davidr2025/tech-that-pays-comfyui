Funding boost for paramedics, ambulance service in Ontario region
Ambulance service in Durham Region earned a financial boost of nearly $41 million from the province this week to connect more people to emergency care faster and increase the availability of paramedics and ambulances in the community. Ontario is increasing land ambulance funding by 10 per cent, bringing the province’s total investment in the region to $40,247,775 this year, an increase in base funding that helps ensure municipalities address increased costs so they can continue to deliver high-quality emergency care. The province is also investing $742,616 in the Dedicated Offload Nurses program to reduce delays paramedics encounter when arriving at hospitals to transfer patients. The funds will be used to hire nurses and other eligible health professionals dedicated to offloading ambulance patients in hospital emergency departments. 9 million investment will help Durham Region continue to deliver high-quality emergency care by supporting frontline paramedic services and reducing ambulance offload delays in our hospitals,” said Ontario Finance Minister and Pickering-Uxbridge MPP Peter Bethlenfalvy. ” This investment is part of the more than $1 billion in land ambulance funding Ontario is providing municipalities across the province this year, representing an average increase of nine per cent from 2025. “During life’s most challenging moments, residents need to know help will be there when they call. This increase to our provincial Land Ambulance Service Grant funding and Dedicated Offload Nurses program recognizes the growing needs of our communities,” said Region of Durham Chair John Henry. The Dedicated Offload Nurses program allows paramedics to get back out into the community faster and respond to their next 9-1-1 call sooner and has played a significant role in reducing ambulance offload times and increasing ambulance availability for 9-1-1 patients across the province.

Support is moving closer to home for some Brampton cancer patients
For people going through cancer treatment, getting support does not always mean another trip to a hospital or specialized health-care facility. Now, some of that support is coming to neighbourhood community spaces across Brampton. Wellspring Chinguacousy is expanding its free cancer exercise and movement programs beyond its cancer support centre, bringing services to Century Gardens Recreation Centre and the Cyril Clark Library Branch. The expansion is being supported by a $475,900 Ontario Trillium Foundation grant that will fund the initiative over the next three years. For Wellspring, the change is about making it easier for cancer patients and their families to access support during what can be a physically and emotionally difficult time. “We provide services to about 600 families annually, and this expansion will enable us to reach more cancer patients and enhance access to our programs,” Wellspring Chinguacousy executive director Rebecca Harrison said. ” Cancer Exercise is being offered at Century Gardens Recreation Centre, while Tai Chi and Yoga programs are being offered at the Cyril Clark Library Branch. The programs are part of Wellspring’s broader approach to helping people deal with the physical, emotional, social and practical challenges that can accompany a cancer diagnosis.
