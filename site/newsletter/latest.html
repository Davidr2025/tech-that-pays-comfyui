El Niño to bring milder temperatures, and more potholes, to Mississauga
Mississauga officials say a potentially historic El Niño that’s expected to bring milder temperatures to southern Ontario this fall and winter will also impact the city in other ways. Coupled with climate change, “which is warming ocean waters,” the El Niño effect could be even stronger, the City of Mississauga said this week. “In Mississauga, that could mean a slower start to winter and more days when temperatures rise above and fall below freezing,” city officials said Thursday, adding some city services “may be delayed or look different than usual” as a result. “A strong El Niño could bring a longer, milder fall and a higher chance of extreme weather to Mississauga,” the city continued. Beyond Mississauga and southern Ontario, weather experts have said El Niño, a natural weather pattern caused by warmer-than-normal ocean temperatures that can affect global weather, will also have a strong impact across much of Canada in the coming months. ” Residents should become familiar with the municipality’s online emergency preparedness guide, officials added.

Coroner’s inquest into death of Jamal Francique postponed by a day
Last Updated November 12, 2026 at 10:47 am A coroner’s inquest meant to examine the circumstances surrounding the death of a man who was shot in the head by police west of Toronto nearly seven years ago has been postponed by a day. The inquest into the death of Jamal Francique was scheduled to begin Thursday morning, but a notice on the inquest website says it will now start Friday. The notice says details on the delay will be provided at that time. Francique died in hospital on Jan. 9, 2020, two days after police shot him during a botched arrest in Mississauga, Ont. He was 28. About a year later, Ontario’s police watchdog cleared the Peel regional police officers of wrongdoing, saying the officer who opened fire reported fearing for his life when Francique drove at him. The Special Investigations Unit said there were aspects of the officer’s behaviour that gave rise to questions, however, such as the decision to place himself in the vicinity of a fleeing driver. The SIU said police were investigating Francique for allegedly dealing drugs and possessing a firearm at the time, but couldn’t confirm those allegations so they decided to arrest him for allegedly breaching bail conditions. The agency said plainclothes officers in unmarked cars waited for Francique to get into his car outside his home.
