38 Closures Scheduled Across Six Peel Highways on Aug. 13
According to Mississauga.com, the province has scheduled 38 separate closures across provincial highways in Peel Region for August 13, 2026. The affected routes include Highway 10, Highway 401, Highway 403, Highway 410, Highway 9 and the Queen Elizabeth Way, the same corridors that carry most commuter and commercial traffic through Mississauga and the surrounding region. Mississauga.com reports the closures are tied to short-term or emergency repairs and general highway maintenance rather than a single large construction project. With that many highways affected at once, the outlet advises drivers to plan their route before heading out rather than assume their usual path will be open. Mississauga.com also points to a separate closure list for anyone travelling to highways outside Peel Region. Anyone driving through the area Wednesday should expect at least some lane restrictions or detours as crews carry out the scheduled work.

Metrolinx Says Hazel McCallion LRT's Civil Work Won't Wrap Until Next Year
According to INsauga, construction on the Hazel McCallion LRT in Mississauga is expected to continue for at least another year, with the 18-kilometre light rail line ultimately running 19 stops along Hurontario Street from Port Credit GO to the Brampton Gateway Terminal at Steeles Avenue. Metrolinx president and CEO Michael Lindsay provided the update during a Tuesday announcement marking the completion of tunnelling on the separate Eglinton Crosstown West extension, saying 75 per cent of the LRT's track work is finished, eight of 14 traction power substations are energized and the maintenance and storage facility is already powered. Lindsay said Metrolinx hopes to have all civil infrastructure on the project complete by roughly this time next year, after which the line would move into its testing phase. INsauga reports Mississauga officials have long expressed frustration with the project's delays, saying the construction has hurt local businesses and tested drivers' patience daily. One city official told INsauga it wouldn't surprise her if the LRT didn't carry its first passengers until 2029.

Dixie Curling Centre Turns Newcomers Into Curlers With a Five-Week Crash Course
According to INsauga, curling is drawing growing interest across Ontario, partly thanks to its Olympic profile and rising accessibility, and Mississauga's Dixie Curling Centre at Applewood Heights Park has built its programming around welcoming people who have never tried the sport. INsauga reports the six-sheet facility runs programs 14 hours a day, seven days a week during curling season, offering everything from beginner lessons to novice, junior and advanced men's, women's and mixed leagues. The centre's adult Learn-to-Curl program is a five-week course where instructors provide all the necessary equipment, including grippers, sliders, brooms and delivery sticks, and teach delivery, sweeping, basic strategy and etiquette. INsauga notes the centre also runs a Little Rocks program for kids as young as seven, offering two hours of on-ice instruction and gameplay plus snacks, with a path toward bonspiels and competitive teams. That youth program costs under $300 for 20 weeks of two-hour weekly sessions, making it one of the more affordable ways for young Mississauga residents to try the sport.

A Logistics Fight With the City Sinks Port Credit's First-Ever Renaissance Faire Before It Opens
According to INsauga, the Port Credit Renaissance Faire, which was set to bring medieval combat, horses, birds of prey and vendors to Port Credit Memorial Park on Aug. 29 and 30, has been cancelled before it ever took place. INsauga reports the event would have marked the first time a Renaissance faire was held in Mississauga, but the organizer said logistical issues with the City of Mississauga forced her to pull the plug. She told INsauga she called the city directly to cancel, describing a breakdown over working out the logistical and financial requirements needed to hold the event, including rules around a mobile business licence for vendors. INsauga reports the organizer said unanticipated additional costs became difficult to absorb on top of other expenses like park rental, insurance, fuel and advertising. She said she had already spent money on advertising and had lined up vendors who were interested in taking part before the cancellation.

A Mississauga 16-Year-Old's Healthcare Nonprofit Now Reaches 106 Countries
According to INsauga, Mississauga teenager Adil Mukhi has built a global organization called Dr. Interested that helps students explore careers in healthcare and medical research, and the group is holding a conference in Mississauga this weekend for local youth. INsauga reports the organization now reaches 106 countries and has impacted more than 160,000 people, according to Mukhi, who serves as its founder and managing director. Mukhi, now 16 and entering Grade 12 in the International Baccalaureate program at Glenforest Secondary School, started Dr. Interested when he was just 13 years old and in Grade 9. INsauga reports the idea grew out of his own experience in Grade 7 and 8, when he felt career education was limited during pandemic lockdowns and had to explore future career paths largely on his own with the help of a few mentors. One mentor helped him pursue his own interest in a healthcare career, and Mukhi said he realized many other students didn't have that kind of guidance, which is what led him to start the organization. “But I realized a lot of people didn't have this, and so that's what the organization came out of, as a way to help people learn about careers,” he said.

Mayor Parrish Leads a Mississauga Delegation to Ottawa With One Message: Fairness
According to the City of Mississauga, city leaders are preparing to travel to the 2026 Association of Municipalities of Ontario conference in Ottawa, running Aug. 16 to 19, to press provincial ministers and opposition leaders on funding, housing and transit priorities. The city says its delegation will include Mayor Carolyn Parrish, Deputy Mayor and Ward 8 Councillor Matt Mahoney, Ward 3 Councillor Chris Fonseca, and senior staff including City Manager and Chief Administration Officer Geoff Wright. The City of Mississauga reports this year's advocacy push is being guided by the theme Fairness for Mississauga, aimed at addressing what officials describe as longstanding provincial funding challenges facing one of Ontario's largest municipalities. Scheduled meetings during the conference will give the delegation a chance to raise those priorities directly with provincial decision-makers and build on existing government relationships. The city notes more detail on its position is laid out in its 2026 provincial pre-budget submission.
