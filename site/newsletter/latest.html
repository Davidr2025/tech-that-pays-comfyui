Twin 16-Storey Towers Now Proposed for Dundas Street Plaza Redevelopment, Down From 18 Storeys
According to INsauga, a proposal to redevelop a plaza at 1225 Dundas Street East, just west of Dixie Road, has been revised again as it heads back for review next month. INsauga reports the site currently houses several businesses, including Danforth Food Market, Baituna Furniture and Spoon and Fork. The project has changed shape more than once since developers first applied in 2022 for a 12-storey apartment building with 462 units, ground-floor retail and 34 townhouses. By 2024, according to INsauga, the plan had grown to an 18-storey terraced building with two point towers connected by a shared nine-storey podium. The latest version submitted to the City of Mississauga scales that back to two 16-storey apartment buildings connected by a six-storey podium, INsauga reports, with developers Stephen-Mitchell Reality Limited, Whitehorn Investments Limited, Lynrob Investments Limited, Richco Investments Limited and Tobdele Investments Limited behind the application.

1930 Meadowvale Village Bridge Gets Restoration Work as Wider Road Study Continues
According to INsauga, Peel Region has closed one lane of the Old Derry Road bridge in Mississauga's Meadowvale Village area from today through Friday to allow restoration work on the structure. INsauga reports the bridge crosses the Credit River between Historic Trail and Credit Valley Conservation Road, with traffic control measures in place in the area for the next three days. The bridge, built in 1930, is a designated heritage structure within the Meadowvale Village Heritage Conservation District and serves as a scenic gateway into the historic community, according to INsauga. The restoration work comes as the City of Mississauga carries out a broader environmental assessment study for Old Derry Road, launched earlier this year, INsauga notes. That study is considering improvements including pavement rehabilitation, new cycling infrastructure and the repair or replacement of existing bridge structures along the road, including the Old Derry Road bridge itself.

Conditional Passes Issued to Six Mississauga Eateries After Peel Health Inspections
According to Mississauga.com, Peel Public Health issued conditional passes to six Mississauga restaurants during recent food safety inspections. Mississauga.com reports Peel Public Health inspectors routinely check restaurants, cocktail bars, beverage rooms, bakeries, takeout establishments and other food businesses across the region to ensure compliance with safety standards. A green pass sign, according to the report, indicates a business is meeting the requirements set out under Ontario's Health Protection and Promotion Act for food safety and operational standards, while a conditional pass signals violations that inspectors found during a visit. Among the six restaurants cited, Mississauga.com reports Kwality Grand Indian Buffet on Courtneypark Drive was inspected on August 18. The outlet notes the routine inspection program is intended to give residents a way to check on the food safety record of restaurants and other establishments before dining out.

Mayor Parrish Says AMO Conference Meetings Secured New Housing Funding for Mississauga
According to INsauga, Mississauga Mayor Carolyn Parrish says a series of meetings in Ottawa this week has secured new provincial funding for housing in Mississauga and Peel Region, though she did not disclose the exact amount. INsauga reports Parrish described the city's delegation as well received at the 2026 Association of Municipalities of Ontario conference, where she was joined by Deputy Mayor Matt Mahoney, Ward 3 Councillor Chris Fonseca, City Manager Geoff Wright and other senior city officials. The delegation met with Ontario cabinet ministers, opposition party leaders, MPPs and industry stakeholders to advocate for Mississauga's priorities, according to the city. Beyond affordable housing, INsauga reports those priorities included strengthening public transit, supporting economic growth and trade resilience, addressing flood prevention and infrastructure needs, securing a fairer police funding formula for Peel, and investing in arts, culture and sport infrastructure. City officials also raised concerns about electric scooter safety in Mississauga, an issue INsauga notes has come up repeatedly at council over the past two years.

Eight Studios and $1 Million in Annual Revenue Anchor Mississauga's Push to Stay a Film Hub
According to Mississauga.com, the city is doubling down on its identity as a film and television production hub as growing incentives in the United States put pressure on Ontario's industry. Mississauga.com reports the city is home to eight major film studios with more than one million square feet of combined stage space, including CBS Stages Canada on Matheson Boulevard East. The city earned close to one million dollars in revenue from film and television production in 2021, and officials describe that activity as an important contributor to the local economy. In June 2026, according to Mississauga.com, city council adopted a strategy called The Creative Advantage, a transformational action plan meant to increase Mississauga's appeal to creative businesses and investment, including in film. Industry officials cited by Mississauga.com caution that the broader competitive landscape facing the sector will still be shaped largely by policy and investment decisions made at the provincial and federal levels, not just by the city's own initiatives.

Paul Coffey Park's Flight Garden Gets a Full-Size Tribute to Malton's Avro Arrow
According to the City of Mississauga, a replica of the Avro Arrow CF-105 has been permanently installed in the Flight Garden at Paul Coffey Park, with an unveiling ceremony planned in partnership with the Malton Business Improvement Area. The city says Mayor Carolyn Parrish, Ward 5 Councillor Natalie Hart, other members of council, Malton BIA chair Sam Kohli and project sponsors are set to take part in the outdoor ceremony marking the jet's official unveiling. According to the city, the project commemorates the supersonic aircraft that was designed and built in the 1950s in Mississauga's Malton neighbourhood, once the site of the Avro aircraft plant. The City of Mississauga notes the original Avro Arrow was the largest plane of its kind when built, measuring 55 feet wide and 72 feet long, and was known for its superior speed, lower weight and ability to carry more payload than comparable aircraft of its era. The city also highlights several firsts credited to the Arrow's design, including early use of digital computers and computer numerical control machining, along with computer-controlled navigation, weapons release and electronic countermeasures technology.
