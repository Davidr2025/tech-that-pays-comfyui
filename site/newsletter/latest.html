Arrest Made in Fatal Hit and Run in Mississauga
Region of Peel – Investigators from the Major Collision Bureau (MCB) have arrested and charged a man following a fatal hit-and-run collision in Mississauga that claimed the life of a pedestrian. On Tuesday, Sept. , police responded to the intersection of Winston Churchill Boulevard and Dundas Street after a pedestrian was struck by a vehicle that failed to remain at the scene. Sadly, the victim, a man in his 60s, succumbed to his injuries at the scene. Investigators quickly identified the suspect vehicle as a dark, two-toned Chevrolet Silverado 1500 pickup truck, model year 1999 to 2005. On Wednesday, Sept. 30, investigators arrested and charged 69-year-old Aubrey Lawrence of Hamilton with Fail to Remain at Accident Resulting in Death. The suspect vehicle was also located and seized. Investigators believe the accused interacted with another motorist in the parking lot of a nearby Home Depot shortly after the collision. Investigators are looking to speak with this individual, as they may have information that could assist with the investigation. Anyone with dashcam footage of the collision or surrounding area, or anyone with information about this incident, is asked to contact MCB investigators at 905-453-2121, ext. 3710. This is a tragic loss of life, and leaving the scene of a fatal collision is a serious criminal offence. We all have a responsibility to keep our roads safe and to remain at the scene of a collision.

Mississauga Turns to Licence Plate Cameras to Catch Drivers Who Speed Off From School Zones
According to Insauga, Mississauga's parking enforcement division began using Licence Plate Recognition technology on Oct. 1 to crack down on drivers who park illegally in school zones and drive off before an officer can reach their vehicle. The cameras scan license plates of illegally parked cars, and the city then mails infraction notices directly to the registered vehicle owner rather than relying on a ticket placed on the windshield. City officials say the system is meant to help in spots where a physical notice isn't practical, such as school zones and time-limited parking areas, though officers will keep placing tickets by hand when that's still workable. The technology is rolling out first in school zones to improve road safety and traffic flow around schools, with the city planning to expand it to additional locations in 2027. The move follows a round of parking fine increases across Mississauga that took effect in April 2025, when the penalty for illegally stopping in a school zone jumped to $145 from $65. City officials frame the cameras as a way to both improve safety for enforcement staff and make ticket delivery more consistent. The rollout is one of several changes introduced this month within the city's parking enforcement division.

New Canadian Play 'the joshis' Brings Diwali-Set Family Drama to Mississauga Stage This October
According to Modern Mississauga, SAWITRI Theatre Group has teamed up with Very Serious Theatre Company to stage the joshis, a new Canadian play by Max Kingsbury, running Oct. 22 to 30 at AN1 Studios in Mississauga. The story follows a family gathered to celebrate the festival of lights, with the celebration taking a dramatic turn that changes their lives. The production is directed by Devansh Shah and Raina Desai and carries a content warning for strong language, with a recommended minimum age of 14. Jasmine Sawant, SAWITRI's founder and artistic co-director, says the company is redefining how culturally rooted stories can move, challenge and unite contemporary audiences. Founded in 2003 and based in Mississauga, SAWITRI is an award-winning not-for-profit that has produced more than 93 works, including 23 full-length plays across English, Marathi, Gujarati and Hindi. The company also runs the Mississauga Multilingual Fringe Festival, which it describes as the only multilingual fringe festival in North America. SAWITRI's operations receive partial funding from the City of Mississauga and the Ontario Arts Council.
