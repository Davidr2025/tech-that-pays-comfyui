Ontario to Require Licences and Insurance for Motorcycle-Style E-Bikes Next May
According to insauga.com, Ontario Transportation Minister Prabmeet Sarkaria announced that drivers of motorcycle-style e-bikes will need a driver's licence, registration and insurance to operate on provincial roads starting next May. Sarkaria said vehicles that look like e-bikes but function more like motorcycles pose serious risks to pedestrians, cyclists, riders and drivers. Under the new definition, a true e-bike must have a bicycle-style frame, operable pedals and a motor-assisted top speed of 32 km/h, with anything faster banned from bike lanes and subject to the new licensing rules. The province said penalties will include fines of up to $2,500 and vehicle impoundment. Mississauga Mayor Alvin Tedjo welcomed the change, saying residents have called for stronger e-bike safety measures for years as the city grapples with danger on its streets from some riders.

Port Credit's 130-Year-Old United Church Could Become City-Owned Event Venue
According to insauga.com, the City of Mississauga is in the process of buying a more than 130-year-old United Church property at 151 Lakeshore Rd. W. in Port Credit, which has been listed for sale for nearly six months. Then-Mayor Carolyn Parrish announced in July that the city planned to turn the building into an event venue and a permanent home for the Canada Virtuoso Ensemble. The church, most recently known as Christ First United Church, amalgamated with the Clarkson Christ First United Church on Mazo Crescent in 2019 and moved its regular services there the same year. A Spanish-language congregation currently holds services in the Port Credit building on typical Sundays, according to a church spokesperson. The congregation is also planning a Candlelight Service at the Port Credit church in December, the spokesperson said.

UTM Intern's New Series Traces Mississauga's Ties to Japanese Canadian Internment
According to Modern Mississauga Media, a new four-part series is tracing the connections between Mississauga and the internment of Japanese Canadians during the Second World War. The series, titled A Path to Mississauga, was researched and written by Darrion Singh, a University of Toronto Mississauga graduate who completed a Historical Studies internship with Heritage Mississauga from September 2025 to March 2026. Singh's project revisits how Canada declared war on Japan on Dec. 7, 1941, after the attack on Pearl Harbor, and how Ottawa later invoked the War Measures Act to expel Japanese Canadians from British Columbia by early 1942. The outlet reports Singh credits Heritage Mississauga and University of Toronto Mississauga staff for guiding the internship. The series will explore how Japanese Canadians remember this history and its ties to the Mississauga area, Singh says.

Credit Valley Conservation Warns of Unsafe Water Levels Ahead of Weekend Rain
According to Credit Valley Conservation, residents in Mississauga, Brampton and Caledon should stay away from rivers and streams over the next few days as widespread rainfall moves into the region. The conservation authority issued a water safety statement Thursday warning that an approaching low-pressure system could bring over 30 millimetres of rain by Saturday, with a chance of freezing rain in the Upper Credit watershed. CVC said the watershed received no rain in the past 10 days, so soil is dry and flooding of the Credit River and its major tributaries is not expected. Even so, officials warned some watersheds could see higher flows, making areas near culverts, bridges and dams unsafe for anglers, canoeists, hikers and pets. The statement stays in effect through Sunday, and the authority said it will keep monitoring conditions and issue updates as needed.

Peel Inspectors Shut Down a Dundas Street Restaurant and Bakery
According to Peel Public Health, inspectors temporarily closed a Mississauga restaurant and bakery during a routine food safety inspection. Revrea's Grill & Bakeshop, located at 1057 Dundas St., was ordered to close when it was inspected on Nov. 25, the health unit says. Peel Public Health routinely checks local restaurants, cocktail bars, bakeries and takeout establishments to ensure compliance with the Health Protection and Promotion Act. A green pass indicates a business is meeting food safety and operational standards, according to the region of Peel. The health unit did not specify in its public summary what led inspectors to order the closure.

Peel Police Say 'Project Twilight' Suspect Faces Dozens of Charges in Five Luxury Car Home Invasions
According to Peel police, a man already in custody has been charged with dozens of offences connected to five home invasions in Toronto, Mississauga and Brampton between May 29 and June 2. Investigators believe the break-ins were carried out by the same group of three or four suspects, who forcibly entered residences in the early morning hours and demanded vehicle keys from occupants. Several luxury vehicles, including BMW and Mercedes models, were stolen or targeted in the attacks, though no victims were physically injured, police said. Toronto and Peel police coordinated on a probe dubbed Project Twilight and arrested the man along with a 20-year-old woman from Hamilton on June 8. Det. Cst. Nathan Ball told a news conference Thursday that investigators no longer believe the woman was involved, and said the investigation remains ongoing with more arrests expected.
