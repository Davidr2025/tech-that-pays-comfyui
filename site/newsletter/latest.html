A Man Critically Hurt in an Overnight Mississauga Police Shooting Has Died, SIU Confirms
According to CityNews Toronto, a man who suffered life-threatening injuries during an overnight officer-involved shooting in Mississauga has died, Ontario's Special Investigations Unit confirmed to the outlet. Toronto police were in the area conducting an unrelated investigation early Thursday morning when the shooting occurred, prompting a response from Toronto police, Peel police and the SIU. Three people were taken into custody at the scene, and a second man was taken to hospital with minor injuries. CityNews reports investigators say no suspects remain outstanding and the situation is contained. A Toronto police officer was also hospitalized with minor injuries after a collision connected to the incident, though no details have been released about how the crash happened or whether it was tied to the shooting itself. A Toronto police spokesperson would not elaborate beyond confirming officers were in the area as part of an investigation. Peel police say a heavy police presence remains in place and the surrounding neighbourhood will stay closed for several hours while the SIU, which is automatically called in whenever police involvement leads to serious injury or death, carries out its investigation.

From a 45th Overall Pick to Nine Figures, Dillon Brooks's New Deal Tops US$200 Million in Career Earnings
According to INsauga, Mississauga-born Phoenix Suns forward Dillon Brooks has signed a three-year, US$73-million contract extension that will keep him with the team through the 2029-30 NBA season. The fully guaranteed deal follows the best offensive season of Brooks's career and will push his total career earnings past US$200 million once the contract runs its course. INsauga reports that puts the 30-year-old among the highest-paid athletes Mississauga has ever produced, alongside fellow NBA player RJ Barrett, who signed a five-year, US$120-million extension with the New York Knicks in 2022. Brooks attended Father Henry Carr Catholic Secondary School in Toronto before starring at the University of Oregon, where he led the Ducks to the 2017 NCAA Final Four and was named Pac-12 Player of the Year. Houston selected him 45th overall in the NBA Draft, but traded him to Memphis on draft night, where he spent six seasons and earned NBA All-Defensive Second Team honours in 2023 while helping define the Grizzlies' physical style of play. He later signed a four-year, US$86-million deal with the Rockets in 2023 before being dealt to Phoenix two seasons later as part of the blockbuster trade that sent Kevin Durant to Houston.

Matty Matheson's Maker Pizza Picks a 31-Storey City Centre Condo for Its Next Location
According to INsauga, Maker Pizza, the fast-growing Toronto pizza chain co-founded by celebrity chef Matty Matheson, is set to open its second suburban location inside Mississauga's Exchange Signature Residences, a 31-storey condo tower near Square One. The chain's first suburban outpost opened in Thornhill, and INsauga reports the Mississauga location will sit inside the Exchange District, a new residential development in the City Centre neighbourhood. A countdown on Maker Pizza's website points to an opening in just over three weeks, following an announcement the company made earlier this summer. The exact menu for the Mississauga location hasn't been released yet, but INsauga notes the chain is known for slices like the truffle shuffle, a bechamel-based pie called Frank's Best, and a chili-pineapple Tropic Thunder pizza, alongside a classic cheese pie. Beyond pizza, the restaurant also serves chicken wings and garlic fingers. For Mississauga's City Centre, the opening adds another dining option to a neighbourhood already anchored by Square One and its growing cluster of condo towers.

A 200-Year-Old Streetsville Log Cabin Traces Back to the Family Behind Toronto's Leslieville
According to INsauga, Toronto's Leslieville neighbourhood takes its name from George Leslie, one of the sons of a family that once lived in what is now Mississauga's Leslie Log House. The book Leslieville, written by Joanne Doucette and published in 2016, documents how the Leslie family sailed from Scotland to Canada in 1825, landing in Quebec in mid-May, before John Leslie travelled ahead alone to a property in what was then Toronto Township. INsauga reports John Leslie, his wife Esther Beattie Leslie, and their children later built and lived in the log house on land the family leased, and eventually purchased, from King's College, keeping the property in the family for a century. The home was originally built just north of Derry Road in Meadowvale before being relocated in 1994 to its current site at 4415 Mississauga Rd. in Streetsville. Built in 1826, the log house is one of the oldest surviving log homes in Ontario and holds designated heritage status, with the city marking its 200th anniversary this past weekend. The connection means a piece of east Toronto's identity traces directly back to a family whose original homestead still stands in present-day Mississauga.

Loose Screens and Balcony Furniture Are Bigger Fall Risks Than Most Residents Realize, City Warns
According to the City of Mississauga, falls from windows and balconies can cause serious injury or death, though the city says many of these incidents are preventable through simple precautions under its Balcony Basics awareness campaign. The city identifies common hazards as furniture placed too close to windows or balcony railings, windows left unsecured, and screens that were never designed to stop a fall in the first place. Young children face the highest risk, while older adults can be more vulnerable because of mobility or balance issues, the city says. Renters and highrise residents are encouraged to learn how to recognize these hazards themselves and report concerns early rather than waiting for something to go wrong. The responsibility doesn't stop with tenants. The city says property owners, landlords and building managers must also keep safety features in working order and respond quickly once a problem is flagged. Mississauga backs up the campaign with education, inspections and enforcement action aimed at preventing injuries before they happen.
