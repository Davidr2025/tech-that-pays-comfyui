Ontario to tackle lengthy hospital emergency room waits in Mississauga and Brampton
Emergency departments in Mississauga and Brampton are among 26 across Ontario targeted by a new provincial initiative aimed at cutting wait times and easing pressure on hospitals. The Ontario government announced Wednesday (Oct. 7) it is spending more than $47 million over four years to launch an Emergency Department Action Team (EDAT) that will work directly with selected hospitals to identify bottlenecks, improve patient flow and find ways to get people the care they need sooner. Hospital sites in Mississauga and Brampton are included in the initiative, INSAUGA Canada has learned. William Osler Health System’s Brampton Civic Hospital and Etobicoke General Hospital are part of the project, as are Trillium Health Partners’ Credit Valley Hospital and Mississauga Hospital. The initiative comes as the province acknowledges that emergency departments are facing sustained pressure from increasing patient volumes, more serious and complex cases and growing demands on the health-care system. EDAT will work with the selected hospitals to examine how patients move through the emergency department, from their initial assessment and diagnostic testing through admission or discharge. The team will look for the factors contributing to delays, to streamline processes and getting patients through the system more efficiently. The province says the 26 hospitals were selected based on factors including emergency department volumes and the potential for improvements to benefit the greatest number of patients. Operational and performance indicators, as well as representation across different hospital types and regions, were also considered.

Mississauga neighbourhood one of cheapest for homebuyers in the Greater Toronto Area
A Mississauga neighbourhood was one of the cheapest for homebuyers in the Greater Toronto Area last month. As the real estate market remains sluggish, homebuyers could find good deals this fall. But some neighbourhoods have bigger bargains than others. Each month, real estate platform Wahi lists neighbourhoods with the most and least expensive homes. Wahi uses the median home sale prices in GTA neighbourhoods with five or more sales to create the lists. In September, the least expensive neighbourhoods included Mississauga and Etobicoke communities, according to Wahi. North York neighbourhood Flemington Park topped the list with a median sold price of $383,000 in September. Six homes sold, and it took an average of 26 days to sell properties, according to Wahi. Woodbine in Etobicoke is second on the list with a median sold price of $402,000 in September. Five homes sold, and properties spent about 42 days on the market, according to Wahi. Mississauga’s City Centre is the only Peel Region neighbourhood on this list. The median sold price for homes here was $428,750 in September. A total of 30 homes sold, and properties spent about 34 days on the market, according to Wahi. The most expensive neighbourhoods include old Toronto communities and areas known for large properties.

Mississauga maintains highest possible credit rating from Standard & Poor
Stable outlook reflects strong financial management, economic resilience and readiness for future growth. Standard & Poor Global Ratings (S&P), a leading provider of credit ratings, research and risk analysis, has once again awarded the City of Mississauga a AAA credit rating, the highest rating possible. This marks the 23rd consecutive year the City has received the designation, recognizing its strong financial management, responsible budgeting and ability to remain on solid financial footing while planning for growth and taking on new municipal responsibilities. The rating reflects the City’s approach to strategically reinvesting property tax dollars while continuing to maintain and invest in the infrastructure, services, programs and facilities residents rely on every day. S&P noted that Mississauga remains in a strong financial position and is well prepared for the future. The report highlights the City’s careful management of revenues and spending, even amid economic uncertainty, and ability to fund infrastructure renewal and support housing growth through a combination of reserves, responsible debt financing and funding from other levels of government. While economic uncertainty and trade-related pressures continue to affect investment across Canada, S&P highlighted Mississauga’s ongoing ability to attract business investment, particularly in manufacturing, life sciences and technology. The report highlights the City’s diversified economy, resilient property tax base and strong household incomes as key factors supporting long-term financial stability. Mississauga’s strategic location and transportation network, including Canada’s busiest airport, two national railways and five major highways, continue to strengthen its position as an important economic centre.
